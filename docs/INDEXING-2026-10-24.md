# Indexing diagnosis and submission plan — 24 October 2026

## Evidence and limits

The supplied Search Console screenshots show 83 discovered/currently not indexed URLs, three crawled/currently not indexed URLs, three redirects, one alternate with a correct canonical, and one Google-selected canonical disagreement. The three crawled examples are the country guide, US passport editor and sitemap.xml. The US page and country guide were last crawled on 17 October; the US page was subsequently changed on 23 October. These screenshots do not establish that Google evaluated the latest US content.

Live audit on 24 October: all 146 URLs returned by the public sitemap returned HTTP 200, declared their own canonical, had titles/descriptions and one H1/main landmark, and had no detected noindex directive. All were reachable by an HTML anchor from the homepage (including the footer directory). Robots.txt allows public crawling and references the correct sitemap. The priority audit separately checked the response headers, content types and country canonicals. This is a point-in-time external fetch, not verified Googlebot access or Google's selected canonical.

The sitemap is XML and is a discovery resource; it need not be indexed as a search result. Submit it in Search Console's Sitemaps section, not as a priority page for Request indexing. Redirected and correctly canonicalized alternate URLs usually should not be indexed separately. Inspect the unknown canonical-conflict URL before deciding to change it.

There is no evidence here that another domain using similar path names causes this problem. Keep established URLs. Changing slugs creates a migration and does not make a page more valuable. No new keyword variants, blanket noindex rules or mass redirects were added.

The root cause of Google's selection remains unconfirmed. Earlier generic country templates and overlapping search intent are plausible quality concerns, not proven reasons for these exact exclusions. No authenticated current URL Inspection result, Crawl Stats report, search-performance export or verified Googlebot log was available. The 146 live sitemap entries and 155 local fallback entries reflect different runtime content inventories; local fallback counts are not live indexing counts.

## Commercial focus

Keep the previously chosen US, UK, Australia, Germany and France visitor markets. Existing code offers USD, GBP, AUD and EUR pricing and international PayPal checkout guidance. This is not proof that every customer or bank can pay: merchant settings and successful captured orders by market still need account-side verification. No payment was attempted and no customer transaction data was accessed.

Prioritize explicit tasks: “US visa photo editor”, “DS-160 photo size”, “DS-160 JPEG requirements”, “US passport photo 2x2”, “UK passport photo digital size”, and “Australian passport photo checker”. These are intent targets, not measured search-volume or revenue forecasts. Avoid claiming domestic German/French passport downloads replace approved capture or provide official photo codes. For these residents, destination-specific foreign visa tasks are a clearer potential paid use case, subject to the actual application rules.

Measure 28-day and 90-day Search Console page/query results with separate visitor-country filters. Compare relevant landing pages with preparation, checkout and captured purchase data in analytics. A keyword impression, configured currency or opened checkout is not revenue.
